A starting point, not a test

Which Class Should You Try First?

There is no single “best” martial art for everyone. The right choice is one that matches your goal and that you can enjoy and attend consistently. Use this table to choose a sensible first class to explore.

The short answer

Choose by the kind of movement that interests you, the training setting in which you feel comfortable and the student's age — not by what looks toughest. If two options sound right, a trial class and a conversation with the coach will be more useful than any online quiz.

Before a trial class

Three Checks That Make the Choice Easier

  1. Age and experience: Share the student's age and any previous experience so the academy can recommend a relevant group.
  2. A routine you can maintain: Check that the location, group format and class times fit everyday life. Consistent attendance matters more than a theoretically perfect choice.
  3. How the class actually feels: Notice whether the explanation is clear, the challenge is appropriate and you want to return. That is more useful than trends or highlight videos.

You do not need to reach a particular fitness level before getting in touch. Ask what to wear and whether specific equipment is needed. If you have an injury or medical limitation, consult a qualified medical professional when appropriate and tell the coach before training.

Questions people ask before starting

Short Answers for Choosing a Class

Which martial art is best for a beginner?

A beginner can start Boxing, BJJ, Women's Boxing, a Kids program or Personal Training without previous experience. The better fit depends more on the movement style and training environment you enjoy than on your starting fitness.

What is the main difference between Boxing and BJJ?

Boxing focuses mainly on stand-up skills: movement, defence, punches, distance and timing. BJJ focuses mainly on grappling and ground work: position, control, escapes and leverage.

Does Lucky Roll13 offer adult MMA?

The current MMA page describes a program for kids and youth. Adults can explore Boxing, BJJ and Personal Training; contact the academy to discuss which available path best fits your goal.

Which option is suitable for someone who prefers a women's group?

The Women's Boxing group welcomes beginners and combines technique, footwork, movement and fitness in a dedicated group for women.

How do I choose a martial art for my child?

Start with the child's age, interests and readiness. Then ask about the appropriate group and arrange a trial. The site lists Kids and Youth programs as well as MMA for kids and youth.

Do I need to get fit before I start?

No specific fitness threshold is required before contacting the academy. Training can progress from your current level, but disclose injuries or medical limitations and seek qualified medical advice when needed.

When is Personal Training better than a group class?

Personal Training suits someone who wants an individual pace, full attention or focused work on a particular technical goal. A group class adds training partners, routine and community.

What is the best way to decide?

Choose one or two options that interest you, share your age, experience and goal, and try an appropriate class. A real session makes it much easier to judge what you want to continue.
